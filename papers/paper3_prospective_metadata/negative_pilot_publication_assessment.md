# Negative pilot publication assessment

## Options

### A. Standalone negative-results short paper

Scientific usefulness is moderate: the study has frozen domain shifts,
leakage-audited relations, five paired seeds, shuffled controls, retention
curves, and exact integrity checks. However, novelty is limited because only
coarse equality relations and simple M1/M2 aggregators were eligible. A
standalone paper risks a weak “method did not work” contribution and salami
slicing relative to Papers 1 and 2. It would require broader independently
motivated datasets or a venue explicitly dedicated to negative results.

### B. Methods cautionary appendix in a future Paper 3

This preserves the strongest value: demonstrating why metadata coverage is not
mechanism evidence and why shuffled/retention controls are necessary. It also
provides a transparent gate motivating the prospective acquisition standard.
The risk is that a future paper must genuinely add new data and a new frozen
experiment; the negative appendix cannot substitute for that evidence.

### C. Internal gate only

This is scientifically safe and avoids overclaiming. It loses the opportunity
to document a valuable failure mode for other RF domain-generalization work,
but remains appropriate if no prospectively enriched dataset is collected.

## Recommendation

**Recommend B: retain the completed pilot as a methods cautionary appendix in a
future metadata-enriched Paper 3, conditional on genuinely new prospective
evidence.** If new data are not collected, default to C. Do not pursue A on the
current evidence alone.

Human decisions: target venue appetite for registered negative evidence,
space/budget for an appendix, JamShield/ElectroSense data redistribution and
licence terms, and whether a new collection can be completed without overlap
with the contributions of Papers 1 and 2.
