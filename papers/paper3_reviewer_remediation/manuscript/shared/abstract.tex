\begin{abstract}
Receiver shift can complicate radio-frequency transmitter recognition on unseen hardware. We compare selected source-only and unlabeled test-time procedures on a frozen six-transmitter WiSig task, using 32 physical-receiver leave-one-receiver-out protocols and five seeds. Deployable adaptation methods receive the same deterministic, label-free bank of 128 receiver packets, disjoint from queries by packet ID but not by verified physical capture. No target labels are exposed to deployable methods. Receiver-equal macro-F1 is \val{P0}{macro_f1}{raw} for source-only P0, \val{T3A}{macro_f1}{raw} for T3A, and \val{P2}{macro_f1}{raw} for attentive receiver-context conditioning. The frozen T3A--P0 difference is positive for \dval{T3A_MINUS_P0}{positive} receivers, with receiver-bootstrap interval [\dval{T3A_MINUS_P0}{ci95_lower}, \dval{T3A_MINUS_P0}{ci95_upper}]; P2's difference from P0 is small and uncertain. A post-hoc analysis finds macro-F1 of \val{EMB_STD}{macro_f1}{raw} for source-aligned embedding standardization. Label-dependent oracle stress tests reveal strong sensitivity of T3A to support composition, although peer count changes concurrently and no deployable support-selection rule follows. Source-validation-only temperature scaling improves probability quality without changing classification decisions. These findings are limited to one dataset and deterministically partitioned support, not physically acquired receiver-adaptation episodes or external replication.
\end{abstract}
