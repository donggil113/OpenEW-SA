\section{Results}
\subsection{RQ1: Does Unlabeled Adaptation Improve P0?}
T3A improves receiver-equal macro-F1 from \NPZeroMacroFOne{} to \NTThreeAMacroFOne{}. The absolute paired improvement is \TGain{}, positive on 31 of the 32 receivers. The stored receiver-bootstrap interval is [\TLower{}, \TUpper{}]. The two-sided Monte Carlo sign-flip value is approximately $10^{-5}$; its Holm-adjusted value is unchanged in the later benchmark's one-comparison family. These statistics support consistency across the sampled receivers, subject to the evidence-timing and shared-training limitations in Section III.

Table~\ref{tab:bench} reports all primary benchmark entries, including weaker outcomes. T3A is the strongest evaluated deployable same-information method. This is a descriptive ranking within the frozen candidate set, not state-of-the-art superiority over all RF or adaptation methods.

\subsection{RQ2: Which Evaluated Strategy Works Best?}
P2 reaches \NPTwoMacroFOne{}, a mean difference of only \PGain{} from P0. Its P2--T3A difference is \PvsT{}. P0-WIDE, the source-only capacity control, reaches \NPZeroWIDEMacroFOne{}. The source-only CORAL, DANN, and GroupDRO variants do not exceed P0's mean. This does not establish that source-domain robustness objectives are ineffective in general; they were evaluated under a particular backbone, budget, and domain definition.

The supervised fine-tuning oracle reaches \NSUPFTOneTwoEightMacroFOne{}. It uses the same number of receiver packets but receives support labels, so it must remain outside an unlabeled-method superiority claim. Its modest mean separation from T3A is informative about this particular head-adaptation procedure, not a bound on all possible supervised adaptations.

\subsection{RQ3: How Much Support Is Needed?}
The common-query diagnostic in Table~\ref{tab:budget} reveals strong support dependence. T3A is substantially worse at the smallest bank and improves as the bank grows. \subsection{RQ4: How Heterogeneous Are Receiver Effects?}
The paired view in Fig.~\ref{fig:receivers} retains all receivers in identifier order. T3A's positive mean is not generated by a small favorable subset, although one receiver has a negative seed-averaged difference. P2's positive and negative receiver counts are much more balanced. Hardware-family summaries likewise report positive and negative context effects rather than selecting a favorable family.

These patterns motivate receiver-level reporting. Repeated packets from one receiver cannot supply the same hardware-transfer evidence as additional independent receivers. Nonetheless, LOSO training sets overlap, so the receiver bootstrap remains conditional on this dataset and procedure.

\subsection{RQ5: Does Classification Improvement Improve Calibration?}
It does not uniformly do so. T3A's ECE is \NTThreeAEce{}, compared with P0's \NPZeroEce{}, despite higher accuracy. P2 has ECE \NPTwoEce{}. T3A's NLL is lower than P0's, showing that confidence calibration cannot be reduced to a single scalar judgment. No temperature or confidence threshold is fitted to improve these results.
\begin{figure*}[t]\centering\includegraphics[width=.95\textwidth]{fig05_calibration}
\caption{Classification and probability diagnostics. Left: every receiver for P0, T3A, and P2. Right: ECE and NLL are distinct measures, not interchangeable units.}\label{fig:calibration}\end{figure*}
Figure~\ref{fig:calibration} places accuracy--ECE relationships next to the distinct score measures. T3A changes the classifier's scoring geometry; the tradeoff matters when downstream use needs trustworthy probabilities rather than only a class decision.

\subsection{RQ7: What Did Learned Context Conditioning Fail to Capture?}
P2 performs better with natural receiver support than with shuffled or mismatched donors, but this sensitivity is insufficient to establish useful adaptation. The natural-support mean remains approximately equal to P0 and below T3A. A model can depend on context without using it better than a competent independent or prototype-adjusted comparator.

Post-hoc inference-only query-coupling diagnostics do not materially recover the initial context study's apparent advantage. Shuffled-context source training retains sensitivity to matched support but yields an absolute mean below P0. Oracle composition controls alter multiple methods, including T3A and receiver normalization; they do not isolate a uniquely P2-specific explanation. The equalized matched-ID diagnostic was ineligible under frozen identifiers. These findings are post-hoc and cannot replace the primary V2 decision.
